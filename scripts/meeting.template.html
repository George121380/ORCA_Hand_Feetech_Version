I have assembled an ORCA Hand v1 with Feetech servos. The idea is to build a shared platform for learning useful manipulation skills, starting with a pen.

The current software runs on my Mac. We reuse pinned official ORCA models and the official retargeting solver, with adaptations for v1 and a dedicated Feetech driver. Physical control still needs servo mapping, calibration, and on-hand validation.

02 / THE FIRST OBJECT SKILL

Teach the hand to turn a pen.

First milestone: a 90° turn, held steadily for 0.5 seconds.

What the policy sees

Hand state + pen position, direction, and motion.

What it controls

16 finger joints; the wrist stays fixed.

What earns reward

Turn toward the goal, maintain contact, avoid dropping.

Palm support allowed · free object with contact physicsContinuous pen spinning is a later goal.

The earlier PPO task only tracked joint-angle targets. It validated the training and inference pipeline; direct position control actually performed better. This new task introduces an object, contact, and the risk of dropping it.

PPO is a reinforcement learning algorithm: it tries finger movements in simulation and updates a policy to increase reward. The pen is a free body, moved by gravity and physical contact. There is no scripted rotation or attachment during an episode.

Success requires direction error below 10°, displacement below 4 cm, hand contact, low linear and angular speed, all held for 0.5 seconds. The clip is the single successful trial (seed 20011), independently replayed. It is not representative of reliability.

The policy uses simulator object state. The webcam pipeline tracks the human hand, not the pen, so deploying this policy requires additional object-state estimation.

The mean final direction error decreased from 90.4° for holding still to 43.9° for the latest policy. But only one of 32 trials met the full success criterion, and six dropped the pen. Lower average angle error does not mean the manipulation problem is solved.

The next proposed experiment is a staged curriculum: stabilize the initial grasp, start with small target angles, and gradually increase difficulty. We should compare success and drop rates over varied starts and repeat training with more seeds before drawing strong conclusions.

Proposed next milestoneRepeatable 90° pen turns across varied starting states.

Discussion: which workstream should each of us take?Code & reproducible experiments ↗

I would like us to choose a practical next milestone and divide the work. A reliable 90° pen turn is a concrete first target. Continuous spinning and tool-use tasks, such as turning a screw, can follow once we have stable contact control.

There are three independent areas: simulation learning, perception, and physical integration. For hardware, the driver and safeguards exist, but actual servo mapping, calibration, tendon behavior, and low-speed motion must be measured. For policy transfer, we also need object tracking and a better match between simulated and real dynamics.

The repository includes setup instructions, pretrained experimental policies, validation results, and offline HTML demos. This deck also works offline; only external links and the optional live camera demo need services.
